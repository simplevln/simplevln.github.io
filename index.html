Abstract.

SimpleVLN explores how far native multimodal models can go in vision-language navigation through better formulation, data, and optimization, without task-specific architectural designs.

Read the full abstract+

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) requires embodied agents to ground language in visual observations and make long-horizon decisions. Existing methods often address these demands through task-specific architectures. As multimodal large language models (MLLMs) become increasingly capable, an important question remains: Have we truly unlocked the potential of native MLLMs for VLN before introducing task-specific architecture?

We present SimpleVLN, an architecture-preserving framework developed through studies of navigation formulation, data construction, and optimization. At the formulation level, SimpleVLN combines single-turn decision inputs with fixed-budget global–local memory to efficiently use navigation history without unbounded context growth, while action chunks enable efficient output of multiple primitive actions per model invocation. For data construction, we combine expert demonstrations with policy-dependent DAgger experience and develop a scalable synthesis pipeline that generates large-scale tasks for reinforcement learning. At the optimization level, a progressive IL–DAgger–RL pipeline advances from action imitation to task-level outcome optimization.

Comprehensive experiments validate the effectiveness of these three aspects. We instantiate SimpleVLN across multiple MLLM backbones and model sizes, demonstrating its generality. SimpleVLN achieves 76.7% and 80.1% success rates on the R2R-CE and RxR-CE splits, respectively, substantially outperforming existing methods. These results show that the navigation capabilities of native MLLMs can be effectively unlocked without task-specific architectural designs.

02 — Approach

Adapting native
MLLMs to VLN.

SimpleVLN directly adapts off-the-shelf MLLMs to VLN without architectural modifications. We study native-MLLM navigation from three perspectives: navigation formulation, data construction, and a progressive optimization strategy that shifts from action imitation to outcome-driven learning through environment interaction.

04 — Analysis

Navigation
analysis.

We investigate how navigation formulation, data construction, and optimization affect VLN performance. The following experiments use controlled ablations of these three aspects.

Formulation

Navigation interface and memory design.

(a) Action chunking. Action chunking improves navigation performance, supporting it as an effective action-output representation rather than merely a way to reduce model invocation frequency.

(b) Single-turn navigation. Reconstructing each decision from the current observation and sampled visual history performs better than continually accumulating interaction history in multi-turn dialogue. Although multi-turn dialogue can preserve a fuller interaction history, continually appending observations and actions creates an overly long context that current MLLMs cannot effectively exploit for navigation.

(c) Observation sampling. Uniformly sampling global-history observations performs better than sampling only at action-chunk boundaries or retaining all primitive-step observations, favoring compact, uniformly sampled global history over retaining every frame.

Optimization

Reward design and RL optimization on the R2R-CE and RxR-CE val-unseen splits with Qwen3-VL 4B.

Binary task-completion feedback achieves higher success rates on both benchmarks than an SPL-based reward, supporting simple task-completion feedback as an effective RL signal.

The evaluated DAPO configuration further improves navigation with binary success rewards. However, since the compared configurations differ in more than the optimizer, the gain cannot be attributed solely to the optimization algorithm.
